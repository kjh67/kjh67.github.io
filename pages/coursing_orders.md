### What is the Coursing Order?
In many places, the coursing order is described as the order in which you follow the bells during ringing; while true for simple methods such as Plain Hunt and Plain Bob, this isn't the full story. As its core, the coursing order relates to the order in which the bells ring the leads of a method

### Using the Coursing Order to Conduct!
Using the coursing order to Conduct (capital 'c' intentional!) requires, once mastered, relatively little effort for a very high reward; being able to work out where any other bell should be, at any time, provided you have one 'trusted' bell to work from (things are much easier when your own bell is your source of truth!). Consider for now ringing a plain course of a regular major method, where you are on the tenor, and the 5 goes wrong. Remember that the coursing order relates to the order in which bells ring the leads. In the current coursing order (8753246), the 5 is two positions ahead of you: therefore, if you are ringing 2nds place bell in the method, the 5 will be ringing the place bell two positions after 2nds place bell in the plain course coursing order.

Say some calls have been made, and the (full) coursing order is now 8723456. The 5 goes wrong again. This time, they are five positions ahead of you in the coursing order; therefore, they will be ringing the place bell five positions ahead of yours in the **plain course coursing order**. If you are ringing 8ths place bell, the 5 should be 4ths place bell. (Since the coursing orders are cyclic, you could also reach this answer by considering the 5 to be two positions *behind* you, and then work backwards through the plain course coursing order).

The above paragraph may be slightly mind-bending at first (at least, it was for me!). Why use the 'actual' coursing order to calculate the 'offset', but the plain course coursing order to work out the place bell? It may help to consider the coursing order being rung more explicitly as a transformation of the plain course; in the example above, by whichever calls have been made, the 5 has been transferred onto the line originally being rung by the 4th (compare the coursing orders before and after the calls: 8753246 -> 8723456). We therefore *really* want to know which place bell the 4th would be ringing if we were in the plain course, and therefore use the coursing order from the plain course to find out which place bell the 5 should be.

As briefly mentioned above, the 'plain course coursing order' of 8753246 applies to all methods with *plain bob lead ends* (so-called regular methods); irregular methods with different lead ends will have a different coursing order in the plain course. It goes without saying that the 'plain course coursing order' you use to work out where other bells should be, should be the one which applies to the method you're currently ringing!
